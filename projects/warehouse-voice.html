Don't click.
Just say it.

Warehouse workers pick orders with both hands busy — so the interface has to be voice. At HackUPC 2024 we built SEIDOR's challenge in 36 hours: speak your order, get routed through the aisles.

HackUPC 2024 ↗

"add twelve units of reference 4-7-2" → routed via shortest path

The problem

Hands busy, eyes busy, screens everywhere

SEIDOR — a Spanish IT consultancy with ~9,000 employees — brought real operational pain: workers create purchase orders, then walk aisles collecting items, fighting misplaced products, wrong references and bad quantities. Every screen interaction is friction; every wrong turn is walking time — the dominant cost of picking.

How it works
01

Speak — the worker talks, speech-to-text listens

02

Understand — intents matched against SEIDOR's product catalog: add items, fix a reference, report misplaced stock

03

Route — the pick list maps onto the warehouse layout; pathfinding computes the visiting order (a traveling-salesman-shaped problem)

04

Answer back — spoken response; hands never leave the shelf

The real work

Failure conversations

The challenge graded exception handling, not happy paths — the shelf is empty, the spoken name matches nothing, the quantity is absurd. Designing those conversations was most of the 36 hours.

Speech-to-textConversational AIPathfindingPython
Why it mattered

My first build for a real company's real use case — and the pattern for everything after: find the operational pain, put AI at the interface, let optimization do the invisible work.
