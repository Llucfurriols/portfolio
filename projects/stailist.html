StAIlist

Photograph your clothes; Gemini vision agents clean, classify and catalog them. Then see outfits on an AI avatar of your own body, matched to tomorrow's weather. Built with Oriol Datzira for Google's Gemini Hackathon.

Live demo online Frontend lead & UX Jan – Feb 2026 · 5 weeks · team of 2 Open source · MPL-2.0
Try the demo ↗ GitHub ↗
The problem

No styling app knows what's in your closet

Everyone owns more clothes than they wear. The obstacles are vision problems: garment photos are messy (hangers, backgrounds, folds), outfit choice depends on weather and occasion, and nobody trusts a recommendation they can't see on themselves. Plus the sustainability angle: resurfacing the clothes you forgot beats buying new ones.

The agent pipeline
01

Clean-up agent strips backgrounds and hangers from garment photos

02

Classifier agent extracts structured attributes — type, color, material, season, formality — Pydantic-validated into Supabase

03

Avatar agent turns a full-body photo into a clean avatar (straightened, inpainted)

04

Try-on agent dresses the avatar in your selected garments, photorealistically

My part

The product surface

I led the full Flutter app — dashboard, garment review flow, weather-aware outfit-of-the-day, multi-day trip planning — plus the prompt engineering that forces every selected garment onto the avatar without cropping half of them off. Oriol owned the FastAPI/Gemini backend; the seam between us was a well-defined API.

Hard parts
  • Multimodal outputs are chaos — every Gemini call sits behind defensive parsing
  • Hackathon physics: two people, five weeks, 174 commits, free-tier cold starts — choosing which corners to cut was the real product decision
What I learned

In agent pipelines, prompt design is API design. The difference between an avatar wearing your jacket and someone else's jacket is three stubborn sentences of instruction.
